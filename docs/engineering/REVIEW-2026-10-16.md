# Aerowise review — October 16, 2026

A whole-product UX, UI and workflow review requested by Justin ("brutal, honest"). Eleven reviewers walked a fresh local copy of `main` (`8c7ee06`, demo mode, every workflow flag on) as technician (Pixel 7), office, owner, homeowner, prospect and platform admin, read the code behind what they saw, and one researched Texas ATU rules and competitors online. A second agent re-checked every finding live or in code before it counted. All 222 findings held up (199 confirmed, 23 confirmed with corrections, none refuted), and the checkers found 43 more; they are merged into the ranked list below. Fixes made in the same session are tracked in [the fix log](#fix-log).

## Verdict

Aerowise's foundations are stronger than most solo-built SaaS. Every route has to declare a permission, the technician app really works offline, the ledger math is careful, and its reading of 30 TAC 285.7 goes deeper than generic field-service tools. The parts people actually touch are not ready for a paying Texas provider, though, and a careful prospect would see that within one demo. Two bugs move money wrongly today. 'Renew now' leaves the emailed renewal open, so a homeowner can pay for a term that already exists and the office can bill it a second time. Send and Issue on estimates and invoices silently drop unsaved edits, so the customer gets the old price. The compliance loop the product exists for is one-way and partly blind. The MP signs a statement that they reviewed a report the page never shows, a finished inspection can't be corrected, an override leaves a filing stuck forever, and the demo emails unsigned reports to the 'county' one second after Finish. The field day has gaps a tech hits in week one: no way to record a locked gate or a loose dog, no site history, and failed items or 'pumping needed' create no follow-up. The Android back gesture exits the app and can lose the last answer, and a repair sign-off can read 'follow-up needed: Yes' next to 'No' while the work order quietly closes. The office lacks basic moves: finding a caller by phone number, moving a visit to Thursday, changing someone's role, editing a plan's price, or recording that a customer agreed to texts. The Due and Schedule lists usually show a later date than the county's deadline. The inbox never shrinks, because finished work never closes its issue. Signing out or deactivating a tech leaves customer gate codes and phone numbers readable on the phone. The demo is the main sales tool, and it shows a company that is behind (5 overdue, a 254-mile day, a finished pump-out still flagged High). Its tour opens on a filing page full of hedges and SHA-256 hashes and ends with no way to book a call, and on staging, walkthrough requests go to an outbox nobody reads. The first screen a real owner sees asks them to accept terms that call themselves an unapproved draft. Most of this comes from a few root causes: code paths that meet without a single source of truth, no shared submit/label/date/badge layer, patched global CSS, and copy written like an audit log rather than for a dispatcher. Breadth (estimates, disposal, QuickBooks, Stripe Connect, deposits) has outrun the three-visits-a-year core loop. Before any outreach, freeze new modules and fix the two money bugs, the report review loop, the field outcomes and the demo.

### What each one is and the proposed fix

**1. 'Renew now' leaves the emailed renewal open: the homeowner pays for a term that already exists, and the office can bill that term again** (critical)

Reproduced twice by both the reviewer and the verifier. POST /contracts/:id/renew (contracts.ts:713-766) creates the next term but never touches the open renewal. The homeowner's link still offers the same term and price. They sign and pay. The renewal then lands in 'Needs review: already renewed another way', where 'Activate now' fails and 'Mark declined' would be untrue. 'Bill next installment' then bills the same term again (INV-1005 paid plus INV-1006 open in the verifier's run). After a phone renewal the open offer also keeps sending reminder emails and texts with a pay link. With a 60-day window on 12-month terms, about 1 in 6 contracts has an open offer at any time.

*Fix:* In apps/api/src/routes/contracts.ts /renew, inside the transaction, either refuse with 409 ('Finish it from the renewal page') or set the open renewal to a new 'superseded' status. Run activationProblem() (packages/renewals/src/renewals.ts:235) in POST /renew/:token/sign and /renew/:token/checkout before any invoice is created or charged, and show 'Your plan was already renewed, nothing is due' on the link. Skip superseded renewals in sendRenewalEmail and the renewal_reminder SMS (packages/tasks/src/renewal-task.ts:250-258, sms-task.ts:86-95). Add a needs_review resolution that links the paid invoice line to the successor contract's installment 1 (the same update activateRenewal does at renewals.ts:329-346) or refunds it. Hide 'Renew now' in SiteContracts.tsx while an open renewal exists.

**2. Edits typed into a draft estimate or invoice are silently dropped when you press Send or Issue** (critical)

On estimates, 'Send by email', 'Mark sent' and 'Record acceptance' sit outside the DraftEditor form and act on the last saved draft. On invoices, 'Issue and email' and 'Issue without emailing' sit outside InvoiceEditor and do the same. The editor shows the edited total directly above the button. Verified: Labor changed from $95 to $120, then Send, produced a sent estimate at $95. An invoice edited to $250 was issued at $275 and emailed. There is no warning, issued invoices are frozen, and the office believes the new price went out.

*Fix:* Put the send/issue/accept buttons inside the editor component, or lift editor state so each action awaits save() first (apps/web/app/(office)/estimates/[id]/page.tsx:312-322 with components/EstimateLineEditor.tsx; apps/web/app/(office)/invoices/[id]/page.tsx:164-176 with components/InvoiceEditor.tsx:58-111). Until then, track a dirty flag and disable those buttons with 'Save your changes first'. Add a beforeunload guard on dirty drafts. Add one Playwright test per editor: edit, then send, then assert the sent amount.

**3. The report review loop is blind and one-way: the MP signs without seeing the report, nothing can be corrected, overrides strand filings, and the demo emails unsigned reports** (high)

There is no office view of a held or draft inspection's readings and photos. The held card links only to the site, and GET /inspections/conflicts/:id has no UI. The signature block says 'I have reviewed this maintenance report (version 1)' and links no report. The PDF exists, two clicks away on the site timeline. On the phone, Finish completes in one tap with no review, the record becomes read-only, and the API says 'A completed inspection is final'. The office's 'Accept anyway' override on a blank inspection marks the visit done and moves the visit counter and next due date. The filing then sits in 'Needs review' forever, because the adapter re-validates the raw values (packages/compliance/src/adapter.ts:147-158) and ReportCorrection only appears on rejected filings. In the demo, Example County (autoSubmit) emails the report about 1 second after Finish with 0 signatures, while the tech recap says the office reviews and signs it. mayAutoSubmit (packages/domain/src/filings.ts:519) will also auto-send for any future verified profile.

*Fix:* In components/ReportSignature.tsx, put an 'Open report version N (PDF)' link and a short answer/issue/photo summary above the attestation. On held inbox cards, show 'Phone copy: N of M readings, P photos · View' using the existing GET /inspections/conflicts/:id, and name the visit it will complete. On the phone, add a one-screen exceptions review (fails, out-of-range readings, photos) before Finish, and allow Reopen until the report is signed. Show ReportCorrection on needs_review filings whose problem is missing readings, so the MP can enter what the tech reported, with an audit trail. Limit the override to owner/MP and refuse it in apps/api/src/routes/inspections.ts:905 when required readings (not just photos) are missing. Never auto-submit before the MP signs: gate mayAutoSubmit on a signature and make tx-example-county hold for signature like the real rulesets. The recap should state what actually happened ('With the office for review' or 'Emailed to LCRA at 1:18 PM').

**4. No way to record 'couldn't do this visit' (locked gate, loose dog, nobody home); stuck and missed stops vanish from the office's view** (high)

StopDetail offers only 'Arrive and start inspection'. Typing 'Gate locked, owner not home' into 'Access problem (optional)' and tapping Finish gives 16 required answers plus a photo. The tech can leave a draft stuck at 'In progress', or tap Pass on equipment they never saw, which puts made-up readings into an MP-signed county report. accessIssue is stored on the draft, but no office screen shows it. /schedule shows a cut-off sentence ('started on a phone; it continues on the new technician's'). A stop not done on its day raises no inbox item, drops out of Unscheduled and off the phone, and resurfaces only once it is overdue. Arrive can't be undone, and its time prints as 'Arrived' on the report even if the inspection happens hours later.

*Fix:* Add a job status 'attempted' (packages/domain/src/jobs.ts:6 plus a migration on the jobs.status check). Add a 'Couldn't do this visit' button in apps/web/components/tech/StopDetail.tsx and at the top of the form: reason chips (locked gate, animal, nobody home, owner refused, unsafe, weather, other), an optional photo, GPS and time. Sync it through its own endpoint so no report or filing is created. Raise an inbox item 'Attempted visit, reschedule before <windowEnd>' and count the attempts. Add a 'visit_missed' inbox item for jobs with scheduled_date before org today still scheduled or in progress, with a Move action. Allow 'Not starting this stop' while the draft is empty, or set arrivedAt at the first answer. Finish the sentence at apps/web/app/(office)/schedule/page.tsx:362 and show the draft's accessIssue and last sync on the schedule card.

**5. Signing out or deactivating a tech leaves the route, customer phone numbers and gate codes open on the phone, with a working Start button** (high)

signOut() in apps/web/app/tech/page.tsx:297-304 only calls /auth/logout. openTechSession returns {kind:'cached', reason:'signed_out'}, and refresh() renders the full route for every cached reason. Opening /tech with no session shows 'Tess Tech', all 7 stops, 'Call (512) 555-0101' and 'Gate code 5497', even with the network off. After the owner deactivated Tess, her phone could still start and fill a full inspection, which was queued under her name. That work can only arrive if the owner reactivates her, and nothing tells the owner. The banner also shows 'Sign in again to send your changes' directly above 'All changes sent'.

*Fix:* On an explicit sign-out with unsentFor(tenant) === 0, call forgetTenant() to delete both IndexedDB stores and the saved me. If work is pending, keep only the outbox. When the cached reason is signed_out or other_account (apps/web/lib/offline/tenant.ts:84-90), render a locked card ('Signed out. N changes for Tess wait on this phone. Sign in to send them.') instead of StopList/StopDetail, and disable Start. Show the cached route only for offline or error. In SyncBanner.tsx, show the 'changes saved on this phone' line only when pending > 0, with the count. In the deactivate confirm (settings/users), warn that anything unsent on the phone won't arrive until the person is reactivated.

**6. Repair sign-off can say 'Follow-up visit needed: Yes' and 'Another visit needed: No'; the work order closes and the follow-up is dropped** (high)

The Repair template asks a required 'Follow-up visit needed' question (packages/domain/src/forms.ts:102-103). A separate, visually broken checkbox, 'Another visit is needed to finish this work', drives finalVisit (WorkVisit.tsx:60, :456-460), and it defaults to true (app/tech/page.tsx:286). The customer signs a summary that contradicts itself, and the server closes the work order from finalVisit alone (apps/api/src/routes/work-sync.ts:484). Verified end to end: WO-1002/1005 went to completed with the follow-up the tech asked for silently dropped and no inbox item. The service-call template's 'Needs a follow-up visit' outcome has the same conflict.

*Fix:* Add a template-level declaration (e.g. definition.followUp: {field, values}) in packages/domain/src/forms.ts. Derive finalVisit from it in one domain function used by WorkVisit.tsx and by work-sync completion. Hide the separate checkbox when the template declares the field. Block completion if the two ever disagree, and create an office inbox item 'Follow-up requested on WO-xxxx' when a follow-up is asked for. Add an e2e test for it.

**7. Issues don't follow the work: a Fail or 'Pumping needed: Yes' creates nothing, finished work never closes its issue, and the inbox never shrinks** (high)

The verifier finished an inspection with Aerator = Fail and Pumping needed = Yes and got 0 issues, 0 work orders and no inbox item. pumping_needed is read nowhere except the template and the demo story. Separately, completing a work order never resolves the issue it came from; the only write is the manual Resolve at inbox.ts:1092. So a finished, invoiced pump-out stays 'High · Pump-out needed · WO-1001' while the homeowner portal says 'completed'. Scheduled work orders and already-booked overdue visits still show High with a 'Schedule' button. One phone call produces two High cards (dispatch plus complaint), and one imported site can produce three or four.

*Fix:* On inspection completion (apps/api/src/routes/inspections.ts, completed branch near :548), open a repair_needed issue for each failed pass/fail item and a pump_out issue when pumping_needed is true, unless the tech already added one of that type. On the phone, expand an inline 'What's wrong?' with a pre-filled issue when an item is set to Fail. In /work-orders/:id/complete (work-orders.ts:707-728) and field completion, resolve the linked issue and service request in the same transaction, and backfill existing data. In inbox/page.tsx:105-110, render the work order's status on its button. In inbox.ts, drop severity when the work order is scheduled and skip it once completed. Show 'View visit' instead of 'Schedule' on overdue items that are already booked. Merge the service-request and complaint items into one card with both actions, and group cards by site.

**8. Due and Schedule lists show a later date than the county's deadline; one site shows three different 'next due' dates** (high)

/due and the Schedule's unscheduled list sort by service_obligations.due_on, which comes from the contract calendar. The filing's next_due_on is the completed date plus the authority interval (packages/compliance/src/filings.ts:291). Because visits usually happen before their due date, the Due list usually shows a date later than the legal one (Inbox 'by 2026-10-25' vs Due 'Nov 5'). Only the inbox's 30-day check catches it. The tour site shows Feb 12 on the tech card, Feb 15 on the old filing and Feb 16 on the new one, and raises a High alarm four months early. The tech stop shows only the contract due date, not windowEnd.

*Fix:* Have /obligations/due (apps/api/src/routes/contracts.ts:845) return effectiveDueOn = least(o.due_on, latest filing next_due_on) and sort by it. Better, when a filing is created, pull the next open obligation's due_on and window_end forward to next_due_on so only one date exists. Show that one value on the tech card ('Must be done by …' when within 7 days), Due, the schedule cards, the inbox and the filing. Don't raise 'no visit booked' as High more than about 30 days before that date.

**9. Inspection rules miss TCEQ's statewide floor: a 0.05 mg/L chlorine reading on a spray system is filed without a flag, and the form doesn't match the TCEQ record** (high)

30 TAC 285.91 Table IV requires a 0.1 mg/L chlorine residual (or fecal coliform under 200 MPN/100 ml) at every test for surface application, plus an annual BOD/TSS sample for non-single-family sites. The only check in the code is `<= 0`, and only for Hays (inbox.ts:404). A 0.05 reading at a spray site went straight to a submitted filing, and no inbox item was raised. The system's disposal type is free text that the rules never read. Drip systems are still asked about the spray field. The default template has no 'access ports re-secured', no sludge measurement in the trash or aeration tank, no chlorine test method, no tag-punched check and no spray-timer check. BOD/TSS/pH lab boxes sit on the tech's phone.

*Fix:* Add a statewide floor layer that every ruleset inherits. When disposal is spray, flag chlorine below 0.1 on the phone as it is typed (a warnBelow on the template field, using the existing invalidNow hook at InspectionForm.tsx:121) and open an inbox item 'Below TCEQ minimum: fix, retest, report'. Make disposal a real enum that drives the rules, and add UV with a fecal coliform field. Add the annual BOD/TSS requirement through the existing labTests machinery (packages/compliance/src/lab-tests.ts) keyed on use category and surface disposal. Add 'Access ports secured', pretreatment/aeration sludge, chlorine test method and 'tag punched' to the default template in packages/domain/src/inspections.ts. Turn on the spray timer check per site. Hide spray fields for drip. Move the lab values to office lab results.

## Strategy

- Breadth has outrun the core loop. About 67k lines of TypeScript cover estimates, deposits and refunds, disposal logs, plan versions with coverage rules, Stripe Connect, QuickBooks, SMS and 21 rulesets. Meanwhile the loop that runs three times a year per customer (tech finishes the stop, office reviews, MP signs, county receives, homeowner sees and pays) is blind, one-way and slow. Freeze new modules until a pilot provider asks for one, and spend the next cycle on ranks 1-11 and 20-21.
- Price matches Fieldbase On-Site, so providers will choose on daily speed. A five-minute demo claim worth building toward: 'Your tech finishes a stop in under a minute, even with no signal, and at 5 PM you sign and send every report in one click.' Today, Fieldbase's headline feature (one-click county filing) is the thing Aerowise lacks. Offline-first, homeowner self-serve renewal and payment, complaints carried onto the report, and the rolling-date check are real advantages, but they only count once the basics are faster than paper.
- Compliance knowledge is table stakes, and Aerowise's version is unverified everywhere. Make 'How do you submit reports to your county today?' a required onboarding question that settles most of the 143 open ruleset items, and get one authority (LCRA, which takes email) to confirm acceptance in writing before claiming 'filing methods built in'. Stop the homepage overclaiming until then.
- Many of the worst bugs happen where two code paths meet without a single source of truth: Renew now vs the open renewal, the follow-up question vs the finalVisit checkbox, work-order completion vs its issue, filing next_due_on vs obligation due_on, the auto-renew flag vs the signed PDF. Add a short list of domain invariants (one next-due date per site, one open renewal per contract, no open issue on a completed work order, the signed text equals the shown text) with tests that run the paths that cross.
- Most UI defects come from about six missing shared pieces: useSubmit/SubmitButton, ApiError issues with field errors, StatusBadge plus label maps, lib/format for every date and plural, a responsive DataTable, and route error boundaries, along with a split, token-based globals.css. Add a formatter first so the refactors are reviewable, then build these and let agents reuse them. Otherwise every new agent-written page will reintroduce the same bugs.
- The product's voice is a compliance ledger (hashes, message IDs, 'does not assert authority acceptance') even though Justin's own rule is confident and plain for customers, with caveats in the legal pages. Do one deliberate copy sweep across the tech app, filings, work orders, portal and emails, and move the evidence and hedges into collapsed 'Proof' sections and the legal pages.
- The demo is the sales tool and currently argues against the product: a company that is behind, a broken-looking route, a tour that starts on legal hedging, no next step, and lead capture that goes nowhere on staging. Treat the demo seed and tour as a product surface with its own acceptance checks (no red on first screens, the loop completed in five steps, a booking link always visible).
- Model the real customer: a 1-5 person company where the licensed owner also inspects, the spouse runs the office, and people change roles. Role changes, deactivation handover, an owner path to the phone app, and a coherent role matrix (techs see plan prices but not estimates today) matter more than self-serve signup, which can stay white-glove for the first five design partners.
- Field reality should drive the technician roadmap: attempted visits, parts and chlorine billing, site history, on-site quotes, a TCEQ-floor chlorine check, and fast all-normal entry. docs/product/UX-FLOWS.md already specifies several of these (access issue option, last inspection issue, prior readings), so the gap is execution, not discovery.
- Pre-outreach checklist, beyond the code fixes: counsel-reviewed terms with no draft preamble, a real domain and email, lead mail delivered independently of DEMO_MODE, indexing allowed on public pages, 90 days as the default free period, a healthy demo seed, and ranks 1-2 fixed. Consider per-company module toggles (aerobic only, pumping, repairs, estimates) so a new owner sees 6-8 menu items, not 16.

## Per-area verdicts

### Accounts, sign-in, roles and permissions (`auth`)

The server side is solid. Every route has to declare a permission, roles live in one matrix, tokens are hashed and single-use, password reset signs you out everywhere, sign-in errors don't reveal which emails have accounts, and support access is truly read-only and audited. The parts people actually touch are thin. There is no sign-up; Justin creates every company by hand. You can't change anyone's role. A person in two companies can never reach the second one. Nobody can change their own name, email or password while signed in. On the phone, signing out (or being deactivated) leaves the last route on screen: customer phone numbers, gate codes, and a working "Arrive and start inspection" button, with no sign-in needed. The ?next= check after sign-in can be bypassed to send people to another website. The roles don't match how a small company works: a technician who taps an office link lands in the full office screens with dead-end errors and no way back to the phone app, and an owner who does their own inspections has no link to the phone app at all. For one company whose staff never change this works. The first time a tech quits, someone gets promoted, or a phone is shared, it breaks.

Works well:

- Deny-by-default API: every route must declare exactly one of permission/public/platformAdmin or the server refuses to start (apps/api/src/auth/plugin.ts), and the role->permission matrix lives in one file (packages/domain/src/roles.ts).
- Signed-out deep links work: /invoices?status=unpaid, /settings/users, /customers and /tech all redirect to /login?next=..., and after sign-in you land back on the exact page with its query string.
- Login errors don't reveal accounts: a wrong password and an unknown email both say "Email or password is incorrect", a dummy hash keeps the timing equal, and forgot-password always answers "If that email has an Aerowise login, a reset link is on its way. It works for an hour."
- Password reset is done right: the token is hashed, works once, lasts 60 minutes, and reusing it says "This reset link is not valid or has expired. Ask for a new one." Resetting revoked another signed-in session for the same person (its /auth/me went 200 -> 401).
- Invitations: a clear "Join <company>" page; the browser enforces minLength=10 and mismatched passwords are caught; a used link says "This invitation was already used. Sign in instead."; an existing user confirms their current password instead of making a new one; the email is recorded in the outbox and the link can be copied from the Users page.
- Landing pages after sign-in are right: technician -> /tech, owner/office/read-only -> /inbox, and /inbox sends a technician on to /tech.
- Office gets clear owner-only messages instead of crashes: "Only the owner can see the company's Aerowise subscription.", "Only the owner can export the company's data.", and the Company form is disabled with "Only an owner can change these."
- Support view: a clear banner ("Aerowise support view of ... read-only, nothing can be changed. It ends at 1:54 PM. End support view"), writes refused server-side with 403 "Support access is read-only. Nothing was changed.", a required reason, audit events, and End support view returns to /admin.
- Offline sign-ins are kept apart on the phone: each person and company gets its own IndexedDB store, a different person signing in clears the previous one when it has nothing pending, and unsent work from someone else is announced ("N changes made as X ... Sign in as them to send them").
- Cookies are httpOnly and SameSite=Lax (Secure in production), there is a JSON-only CSRF guard, and deactivating someone revokes their sessions at once.

### Technician phone app (`tech`)

The offline engine is the strongest part of the product. I reloaded the page with no signal and the route reopened. An inspection finished offline showed "Done · not sent yet" and sent within about 4 seconds of reconnecting. Starting a stop without signal or without GPS is instant and explained in plain words. The field workflow on top of that engine has real gaps a working tech would hit in the first week. There is no way to record "couldn't get in" (locked gate, loose dog). Work visits have no directions, call button, gate code or dog warning. A routine all-normal inspection takes about 27 taps through roughly five screen heights of scrolling. Finish is final, with no review step and no way to flag a mistake. In Chromium, an answer entered in the last half-second before a phone Back or reload is silently lost. The customer sign-off on a work visit can show two answers that contradict each other. The copy often reads like engineering notes ("Office delivery is shown above", "does not establish authority acceptance"). Many secondary controls are 20–25 px tall. It is a solid prototype, but I would not hand it to a tech in a truck yet.

Works well:

- Offline is real. With the network cut (context.setOffline) I finished an inspection with 2 photos and the stop showed 'Done · not sent yet'. A reload while offline reopened the route through the service worker. Back online, the banner went to 'All changes sent' and the badge to 'Done' within about 4 s (script: scratchpad/tech/offline.mjs, shots tech-off-*).
- Starting an inspection offline opened the form in 43 ms. With location permission off it opened in 64 ms and said plainly 'Location is off for Aerowise on this phone, so this visit is recorded without GPS…' instead of hanging.
- The main controls are big enough for gloves: Pass/Fail 169x48 px, choice buttons 110x48 to 110x86, 'Finish inspection' and 'Arrive and start inspection' 380x56, stop cards 77 to 122 px tall.
- Conditional logic works. Choosing 'Working' for the chlorinator adds the Chlorine level field and the Chlorinator photo. 'Fail' on the aerator adds an 'Aerator problem' photo. The county's requirement made Air pressure required, and 'Reported to Example County Environmental Health' is shown.

### Front-end code health as it affects UX (refactor opportunities) (`frontend`)

The web app has a few solid foundations: useLoad, the offline sync layer, label maps in @aerowise/domain, and an idempotency key on customer creation. The rest of the code doesn't build on them. Each page writes its own submit handler, badge, date format, picker, table and error text, so the same bugs show up page after page. About six shared pieces (useSubmit, ApiError issues, StatusBadge/labels, formatters, query-state lists, error boundaries) would fix most of the screens at once.

Works well:

- components/public/usePublicRead.ts keeps homeowner pay, portal, renew and estimate links from ever redirecting to the staff sign-in page.

- On phones the office nav folds into a grouped Menu, and tables scroll inside themselves instead of making the whole page scroll sideways.

### Domain fit: how Texas ATU providers really work vs. what Aerowise does (`domain`)

Aerowise's reading of 30 TAC 285.7 is unusually deep and mostly correct. It covers the MP signature, owner complaints on the report, the 14-day deadline, the 6-month monitored interval, contract filing and discontinuation notices, and a separate check of the authority's rolling 4-month date. That goes well past what a generic field-service tool knows. But it reads like a compliance ledger built from public pages, not from a provider's day. The things that happen at almost every stop are missing or clumsy. A technician cannot record a locked gate or a loose dog without making up readings. Chlorine tablets and small parts sold on routine visits never reach an invoice. The tech gets no history from the last visit, and a spray-system chlorine reading below TCEQ's 0.1 mg/L minimum goes into a filed report without any flag. The office has no way to review, sign and send the day's reports in one batch, even though "1-click county filing" is the competitor's lead promise. A house sale cannot move a site to its new owner. All 21 real authority profiles are drafts, and for 14 of them, including Harris, Bexar, Montgomery, Travis and Hays, the product does not know how inspection reports are submitted. The price matches Fieldbase On-Site, so a provider will choose on field speed and filing speed. Those are exactly the areas that need work before outreach.

Works well:

- The 4-month math is handled twice, correctly. Contracts get equal visit windows (buildTermSchedule), and the inbox separately warns when the next window opens after the authority's rolling date, e.g. "Next visit starts later than the authority allows" (apps/api/src/routes/inbox.ts:288-305). Electronic monitoring (6 months, 285.7(e)(3)) is modeled with checked and ended dates, Hays's start-anchored schedule is modeled too, and a plan with too few visits gets more before the contract is made.
- Owner complaints and monitoring notices are classified and printed on the authority report, as 285.7(e)(1)(A) requires. Live, the LCRA filing for 1059 Live Oak Ln was correctly held: "1 service request at this site since the last report has not been reviewed for reporting".
- The MP signature model fits 285.7(b)(3). The signed statement names the MP license, the registered technician and the supervision. Only a person with a checked MP license can sign, and the authority and customer copies are signed together.
- Contract filings separate the initial two-year policy, the first contract after it, renewals and replacements (packages/domain/src/readiness.ts:195), and the 30-day discontinuation notice of 285.7(d)(3) is tracked when a contract is cancelled.
- Every ruleset records sources, retrieval dates and open questions. The 14-day deadline (10 for Williamson), per-authority fees and lab-test schedules (Montgomery commercial BOD/TSS) are versioned and frozen per filing.
- LCRA's own O&M cover sheet is filled in automatically, and the submission package is fixed once made.
- The price is competitive. The landing page shows $0.50 per active contract with a $50 minimum. Fieldbase Teams is $50 plus $0.50 per contract (https://www.fieldbaseos.com/), so Aerowise is $50/month cheaper at any size above 100 contracts.
