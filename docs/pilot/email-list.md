# The cold-email list: every Texas aerobic maintenance company found with a published business email

Compiled October 12–13, 2026, from four research passes over public sources only: the county and authority provider lists that print email addresses (Hays 2024 and 2022, Guadalupe June 2026, Williamson May 2026, Hamilton April 2025, Comal's installer page), and the companies' own websites across the Houston region, DFW/North Texas and Central Texas. Nobody has been contacted. Every email is copied as printed on the source named in the row; the odd spellings are the source's. Business listings only: rows that were an individual's name with no company on a county list were left out.

**Count: 151 companies with an address to write to**, in three tiers, plus a list of companies worth calling that publish no email.

## Why these companies, and how the tiers work

The product is for a company that (1) sells aerobic maintenance contracts, (2) has to report each visit to an authority on a schedule, and (3) does that with a spreadsheet, paper or a generic tool. Every row was scored on four things the public record can show:

- **In a county Aerowise has a rule profile for.** The 21 profiles are Hays, Williamson, Travis, LCRA, Comal, Bastrop, Bell, McLennan, Bexar, Montgomery, SJRA, Harris, Brazoria, Grimes, Denton County, City of Denton, Collin, Kaufman, Ellis, Johnson, Hill. A company there can be shown its own county's report on the first call. Companies in other counties are still on the list (their county is next), lower tier.
- **Explicit maintenance-contract or filing language on their site or the county list.** "We file the reports with the county for you" is the exact sentence the product exists for; it's quoted in the Why column where it appears.
- **A size signal.** Two or more licensed providers, a named crew, "serving N counties", review counts. One-person shops are fine customers but cheaper to find by phone.
- **No field-service platform visible.** Across roughly 130 sites, Housecall Pro appeared three times, Jobber once, Service Fusion once, ServiceCore once, and Fieldbase never. The real competitor is a spreadsheet and a PDF contract emailed by hand.

**Tier A** (send first, one personal line each): all four, or three with a company-domain email and contract language. **Tier B**: in a profile county with contract language, but the email is a county-list address or a personal-domain one, or size is unknown. **Tier C**: an email is published but aerobic contracts are not confirmed on their site, the business is pumping-heavy, or the county has no profile yet. Send C last and expect little.

## The honest math and the sending plan

Cold email to small trade businesses gets 1 to 5 percent replies when it is short, plain and specific; a quarter of replies are "no thanks". So **151 emails is roughly 3 to 8 replies and 1 to 4 real conversations**, over two weeks including the follow-up. That is why the list is this long, and why calls are still the main channel: the twenty-conversation number in [questions-before-the-first-call.md](questions-before-the-first-call.md) is reached by phone, not by inbox.

How to send so they land:
- From justin@deshain.com in your normal mail client, one at a time, plain text, no attachments, no tracking links, no bulk tool. A fresh domain sending 100 identical emails in an hour goes to spam for everyone.
- **20 a day, Tuesday to Thursday, 7 to 9 in the morning** (owners read mail before the first stop). Tier A first (two days), then B (three days), then C.
- Pick the body by the company's main county from the five below; change the bracketed bits; for Tier A add the one personal line from the Why column as the second sentence.
- Follow-up once at day 7 (email 2 in [cold-email.md](cold-email.md)), then stop. Reply to every answer within the hour, including the no's.
- Log every send and reply in the Notes column here, with the date.

## What the sweep says about the market, in four lines

1. **Nobody names a filing tool.** About 130 sites say some version of "we file the reports with the county for you"; not one says how. The product's county profiles are the thing they are all doing by hand.
2. **Field software is nearly absent**: Housecall Pro three times, Jobber once, Service Fusion once, ServiceCore once, two home-built portals (Harris County Septic, Ledford), one own app (JB's SeptiLink), one scheduling tool (Van Delden's OnePath). Fieldbase, the "direct competitor", appeared on no site at all.
3. **The payment page is the first software they buy**: PayPal, Stripe, Square, a Newtek bill-pay page, QuickBooks links. That is the door: "you already take payments online; this is the rest."
4. **Harris is the odd county**: every three months, not four. Any email to a Harris company must say so (body 4), or it reads as written by someone who's never filed there.
