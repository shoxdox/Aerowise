# Questions to answer for yourself before the first call

Written October 12, 2026. Short answers, written down, before dialing anyone. Each one changes what you say on the phone or what you can promise.

1. **Who is the first customer, really: the owner-operator with 300 contracts and one tech, or the 1,000-contract shop with a dispatcher?** The product serves both, but the pitch differs: the small one buys "nothing falls through the cracks", the big one buys "my dispatcher stops retyping". Pick one for the first five so the calls are consistent.

2. **What are you willing to do by hand for the first five?** Importing the spreadsheet is promised. Will you also clean it, chase missing permit numbers, drive to the office and sit with the dispatcher, re-key a county form the product doesn't produce yet? Decide the ceiling now, because you will be asked on the call and the honest answer sells better than a vague yes.

3. **How far will you drive?** Hays, Williamson and the lake counties are all within two hours of each other. A provider who gets a visit in week one is a different relationship from one who gets a Zoom link. If the answer is "anywhere in that triangle, once a month", say so on the call.

4. **What is the number after ninety days?** Decided in [pricing-memo.md](pricing-memo.md). If an owner asks and you don't have it, the ninety days stop meaning anything. Don't call before you can answer.

5. **What do you do if the county says no to the sample report?** Ruleset work and a second try, or move on to a county that publishes a channel? The answer decides whether a Hays-only provider (no published channel) is a good first pilot or a slow one.

6. **How many hours a week can you give one provider for ninety days?** Support, the Monday "it didn't sync" call, the data cleanup. Five providers at three hours each is a part-time job. If the answer is "ten hours total", the first batch is two, not five.

7. **Will you put your name and phone number on the website?** The demo is anonymous today. An owner who takes a call from a stranger will look you up. Decide what they find.

8. **What is the one thing you want to learn from the first provider that no agent could tell you?** Suggested: whether the technician's phone form matches how a tech actually works a stop, and whether the county actually accepts the report. Everything else is secondary for ninety days.

9. **What would make you stop?** Three providers in a row saying "Jobber is fine"? A county refusing the format? Decide the signal before you hear it so you don't rationalize past it.

10. **Who is the licensed provider who reads the form, the PDF and the contract text once?** This is the one review no agent can do. It can be the first customer's provider, or someone you pay for an hour. Line one up before the first provider depends on it.
