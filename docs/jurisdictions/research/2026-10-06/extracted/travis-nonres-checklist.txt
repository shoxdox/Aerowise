                                     Non-Residential Development Checklist



Development permit applicants are expected to comply with Travis County code and other applicable public health,
safety, and environmental quality laws and regulations prior to approval. Please complete and sign the checklist below to
determine which additional requirements are applicable to your non-residential project. This list is not all inclusive, and
Transportation and Natural Resources (TNR) reserves the right to request additional information if necessary. All
permits from other jurisdictions must be obtained prior to the issuance of a Travis County development permit.

Environmental Quality

     1. Will the construction project disturb one or more acres, or is it part of a common or                __________
        phased plan of development? (Ex: a small lot in a residential subdivision)

         If yes, a Stormwater Pollution Prevention Plan (SWP3) administered by the Texas Commission
         of Environmental Quality (TCEQ) and Travis County must be implemented onsite, and an
         Erosion and Sediment Control (ESC) Plan with specified temporary and permanent erosion
         and sediment controls to prevent off-site stormwater runoff impacts and permanently stabilize
         the site is required. In lieu of submitting the entire SWP3 for review, please submit a completed
         SWP3 Summary.

         If no, construction sites less than an acre are still required to provide an Erosion and Sediment
         Control (ESC) Plan with specified temporary and permanent erosion and sediment controls to
         prevent off-site stormwater runoff impacts and permanently stabilize the site.

         Reference: Travis County Code Section 482.936
                   TCEQ General Permit TXR150000
                   SWP3 Summary
                   SWP3 Summary Instructions

     2. Will the project add more than 10,000 sqft of new impervious cover?                                  __________

         completion of construction. The PWQCs must be maintained by the owner and will be
         inspected for compliance with an approved Maintenance Plan that is recorded with the County

         Reference: Travis County Code Section 482.917, Section 482.931 & Section 482.944

Endangered Species/Balcones Canyonlands Conservation Plan (Western Travis County only)

    20. For projects located west of Mo-Pac Expressway, have the potential endangered species             __________
        impacts been addressed in compliance with the federal Endangered Species Act (ESA)?

        If no, it is the responsibility of landowners to comply with the federal Endangered Species Act
        (ESA) if there is an endangered species habitat on the property. Landowners who plan to
        develop property in these areas can address ESA compliance by applying for a permit through
        the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service.

        For a faster and less expensive alternative to obtaining a federal permit, Landowners may
        choose the option to comply with the ESA through participation in the local Balcones
        Canyonlands Conservation Plan (BCCP).

        Contact: U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, Austin District Office: 512-490-0057
                BCCP: 512-854-7213 or BCCP@traviscountytx.gov

Wastewater/On-Site Sewage Facilities (OSSF)

    21. Is there an existing OSSF septic system, or will a new septic system be required?                 __________

        If yes, an OSSF construction permit is required prior to issuance of a County development
        permit. An exception may be made if the permit applicant agrees in writing to take full
        responsibility for the OSSF placement on a case by case basis. Also, projects located in the
        areas described below must obtain OSSF approval from the other jurisdictions listed instead of
        Travis County:
             ● OSSF projects within Travis County and within 2,000 feet of Lake Travis (2000 feet
                 from the 691foot contour) are subject to the Lower Colorado River Authority's (LCRA)
                 review instead of Travis County.
             ● OSSF projects within any City of Austin’s Limited Purpose City Limits areas or with
                 frontage on Lake Austin are subject to Austin’s OSSF review.

        Proof of application to the appropriate jurisdiction is required prior to the issuance of a
        County Development Permit.

        Contact: LCRA OSSF Program: 512-473-3216
                Austin Water Utility Development Services Section: 512-972-0296 or
                ossf@austintexas.gov

Commercial Development

    28. Is the project a non-residential development?                                                          __________

        If yes, the permit applicant must send notice to all homeowners and neighborhood associations
        within 1,000 feet of the property where the project is to be constructed, Travis County Fire
        Marshal, and the local Emergency Service District prior to permit issuance. The applicant is

                                                                                                   Rev. 05/11/2023          5
        responsible for providing proof that the notice was sent to the appropriate parties and for
        informing the TNR case manager of any comments received due to the notice.

        The notice and mailing list must be pre-approved by TNR staff and must be sent by certified

          Term                                                     Definition

                           Any man-made changes to improved or unimproved real estate, including but not
Development                limited to structures or other buildings, mining, dredging, filling, grading, paving,
                           excavation or drilling operations

Nonresidential             Construction of subdivisions, streets, and drainage
Construction

                           Construction for a single family residence or mobile home (including septic installation,
Residential Construction   additions, decks, pools, garages, fences, small storage buildings, barns, etc.)

                           Common plan of developments are projects completed in separate stages, separate
Common Plan of             phases and are identified by documentation for the construction project that outlines the
Development                scope of the project that may include plats, blueprints, marketing plans, contracts,
                           building permits, a public notice or hearing, zoning requests, or other similar
                           documentation and activities

Impervious Cover           The total area of any surface that prevents the infiltration of water into the ground,
                           such as roads, parking areas, concrete, and buildings

Critical Environmental     A feature class that includes a bluff, canyon rimrock, cave, point recharge
Feature                    feature, sinkhole, spring, and wetland

Total Disturbed Area       The total amount of area affected by construction activities that include clearing,
                           grading, excavating, or anything else that results in land disturbance

A Driveway vs an           A “driveway permit” is required when an access roadway connects to County ROW.
Accessway Permit           An accessway is an internal roadway and requires a nonresidential development permit.
