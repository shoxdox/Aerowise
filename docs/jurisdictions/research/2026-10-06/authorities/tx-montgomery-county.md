## M2: Commercial/multifamily annual lab testing has order support

Disposition: **public_evidence**. Confidence in source reading: high. Actual authority acceptance: unconfirmed.

Section 10(j) requires annual BOD and TSS for all commercial and multifamily OSSFs. It also restricts aerobic homeowner maintenance to a Certified Maintenance Provider, defined in §10(a)(1) to include TCEQ and county status. The order does not supply BOD/TSS limits or lab filing format in this provision.

Proposed action: Scope annual lab tracking to the explicit categories and actual permit; ask for limits, lab attachments, due-date anchor and submission practice rather than whether the annual obligation exists.

- [County Local Order (live OSSF page link)](../sources/montgomery-local-order.pdf) — PDF pp. 6–8, §10(a)(1), (j); [official source](https://cms1files.revize.com/montgomerycountytx/620145.pdf?t=20250424152017615)

## M3: The initial contract remains a scoped source

Disposition: **partial**. Confidence in source reading: high. Actual authority acceptance: unconfirmed.

The previously cited readable county initial contract requires three inspections each year, one every four months, during the initial two-year period and reports within 14 days after the test. The live site also links new residential/general and food-establishment initial contract scans, archived here for comparison. The adopted order does not settle a separate recurring post-initial monitoring interval in §10(j).

Proposed action: Do not treat an initial contract as proof of renewal-period policy. Confirm whether six-month monitored schedules are permitted after the initial term and what current renewal terms require.

## M5: Jurisdiction exclusions and fee evidence are more concrete

Disposition: **partial**. Confidence in source reading: high. Actual authority acceptance: unconfirmed.

Section 6 excludes existing-rule areas and incorporated cities, adding cities that execute intergovernmental contracts with the county. The live fee schedule has permit/reinspection entries and a $200 septic non-compliance administrative fee per incident; it does not identify a recurring maintenance filing fee. The downloaded SJRA order is a separate authority source, not Montgomery’s order.

Proposed action: Review city/county/SJRA authority against each permit. Ask whether the non-compliance fee can apply to late maintenance filings and obtain the applicable agreement/boundary basis; do not infer a routine filing charge.

## M6: Food-establishment initial contracts require monthly inspections

Disposition: **implementation_gap**. Confidence in source reading: high. Actual authority acceptance: unconfirmed.

The commercial food-establishment initial two-year contract linked by the current All Forms page requires at least twelve inspections per year, one every month, and report submission to owner/authority within 14 days after testing. The scan is an older form (revised November 25, 2009) still linked today. The general initial contract instead says three yearly visits/every four months.

Proposed action: Do not use one residential four-month schedule for all Montgomery sites. Flag food establishments for permit/contract review and model the monthly initial-term schedule when applicable. Confirm whether the older linked form remains controlling and what follows the initial term.
