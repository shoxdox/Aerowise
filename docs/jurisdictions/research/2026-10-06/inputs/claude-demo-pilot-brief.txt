Where things stand
The first brief (docs/PILOT-BUILD-BRIEF.md) is essentially complete: W1–W8 are done per docs/engineering/ROADMAP.md, 13 county rulesets exist as drafts, and the repo is green (670 tests, typecheck and lint clean, checked 2026-10-06). This brief adds three workstreams that stand between "the code works" and "a septic company owner can be handed a link and understand it in five minutes."
The three gaps:
1. No demo a prospect can use alone. Staging reseeds a fictional company, but there is no public demo login, no way to spin up a personalized demo for a named prospect, and no guidance when someone lands in the app for the first time.
2. Nothing has been seen by an outsider. Every screen was built and tested by agents against synthetic data. A polish pass with a prospect's eyes is needed before the first demo link goes out.
3. Ten research packets are not yet rulesets, and the verified workflow has not been used because no county has been called yet. Justin is making those calls; the code must make recording the answers quick.
All ground rules from the first brief still apply, in particular: a draft ruleset never auto-sends, county rules only from cited sources, synthetic data only, and nothing leaves a demo environment.
The relationships the product serves (for anyone new to the domain):
• Homeowner → septic company: pays a yearly maintenance contract; receives report copies, renewals and invoices.
• Septic company → county: inspects every four months, files a report within the county's deadline, files contracts and renewals, pays any county fees. The company is legally responsible for all of it.
• Aerowise → septic company: software that knows each county's rules, prepares the right report, tracks deadlines and proof, and runs the office. Aerowise never files anything itself and never contacts homeowners.
• Justin → county: calls each authority, confirms how it wants reports and contracts, and records the answer so a ruleset can be marked verified.
W10. Demo experience
A prospect should be able to open a link, sign in, and understand Aerowise without Justin on the call. Three layers, built in this order.
W10.1 Public demo
• One fictional company, "Hill Country Aerobic Services" (or similar; all names, addresses and permit numbers made up), with about 300 contracts spread across 3–4 real Texas counties that have draft rulesets, 3 technicians, a week of inspections due, a few overdue, 2 filings waiting for a signature, 1 rejected filing with a correction, renewals in every state, invoices paid and unpaid, a few homeowner texts, and QuickBooks connected to the test company.
• Shared logins shown on the marketing page's "Try the demo" button: owner, office and technician. Passwords rotate on each nightly reseed and the page shows the current ones.
• Reseeded nightly (already exists as pnpm db:reseed-demo); extend the seed to the richer data set above.
• Hard isolation: DEMO_MODE already routes every outside service to its stand-in. Add: no real email addresses or phone numbers can be saved in a demo organization (validation refuses anything not under example.com / +1500555xxxx), and exports are watermarked "demo data".
• A persistent banner: "Demo. Data resets nightly. Nothing is sent to a county, homeowner or bank."
Done when: a stranger opens the marketing page, clicks "Try the demo", signs in as the owner, and can walk the inbox, a site, a filing, a renewal and an invoice without hitting an empty screen or an error.
W10.2 Personalized prospect demos
From /admin, Justin creates a demo for a named prospect in under two minutes.
• Form: company name, logo upload, the counties they serve (picked from the ruleset list), number of technicians (1–15), approximate contract count (100–2,000), owner's first name for the greeting, expiry (default 14 days).
• Result: a new organization in plan_state: demo with synthetic data generated to match: sites placed in the chosen counties (using the test geocoder's deterministic spots inside those counties), the chosen number of technicians with routes, contract count scaled, their logo on the PDFs and header, the company name everywhere. All homeowner names and addresses are fictional; no real-world data is used even for "realistic" addresses.
• A single sign-in link (magic link, single-use to set a password, then normal login) that Justin copies into an email. The link expires with the demo. Role: owner. Optional second link for a technician so the owner can hand a phone to a tech.
• Demo organizations are isolated like W10.1 (stand-in services, no real contact details, watermark) regardless of environment, so they can live on production.
• Lifecycle: demo → expires to read_only after the expiry date, or Justin presses "Convert to trial": the synthetic data is wiped, the organization keeps its name, logo, counties and users, moves to trial or design_partner, and the first-run checklist appears. Deleting a demo removes everything.
• Admin list of demos: prospect, created, expires, last sign-in, pages viewed count, converted or not. Last sign-in and activity are the sales signal.
Done when: Justin creates a demo for "Example Septic LLC" in Comal and Hays with a logo, opens the link in a private window, sees their name and logo on the inbox and a report PDF, every site sits in Comal or Hays, and the technician link opens /tech with a route. Converting to trial leaves no synthetic customers behind. All tested, including that a demo organization cannot send anything real.
W10.3 First-run guidance
• A short guided tour on first sign-in for owner and office (5–6 steps: inbox, a due inspection, a filing and its proof, a renewal, billing). Dismissable, re-openable from the help menu, stored per user.
• Empty states that explain, not just "No items": every list in the office has a one-line explanation and a next action.
• A "How Aerowise handles your county" panel on each site and filing: the authority, status (draft or verified, with date and who confirmed), deadline rule, submission channel, and the open questions if still draft. This is the verified badge surfaced where the owner looks.
• Technician app: a 3-step "today, stop, inspect" intro the first time /tech opens.
Done when: a new owner's first five minutes have a visible path, and a filing screen shows the county's verified status without digging.

W12. Counties: packets to rulesets, and recording Justin's calls
W12.1 Ten packets become draft rulesets
The research packets in county-research-archive/ (Bell, Bastrop, Brazoria, Grimes, Hill, Kaufman, McLennan, Montgomery, SJRA, and the Ellis addendum) become packages/compliance/rulesets/<key>/v1.json, docs/jurisdictions/<key>.md and fixture tests, one PR each, following the existing drafts (Williamson is the model). Rules:
• Only what a packet cites goes in. "Not published" stays null or "unknown" with an entry in profile.unresolved. Quotes that came through a summarizer are marked as such in the source note.
• Where a packet found a real local rule, encode it: SJRA's nine extra report fields and portal channel; Bell's quarterly schedule for food-service sites, annual BOD/TSS and $20 per contract year; Bastrop's $100 late fee and $20 renewal fee; Montgomery's annual BOD/TSS for commercial; Denton County's report email and form fields; McLennan's annual fee and provider penalty.
• River authorities (SJRA) and health districts (Bell, McLennan) use assignment: "reviewed" like LCRA, with the counties they touch listed only to trigger review.
• Each profile's "Open questions for Justin" is copied verbatim from the packet so the admin coverage page shows it.
W12.2 Recording a county call
Justin will call counties and come back with answers. Make capturing them a two-minute job, not a code change.
• On the admin coverage page, each authority gets a "Record a call" form: date, who at the county (name, title, phone or email), and one field per open question, each with the answer and a "resolves this question" checkbox. Saved as a dated note on the ruleset profile (profile.verificationNotes[]), not as rule changes.
• A second step, "Apply answers": shows the current draft values next to the recorded answers and lets Justin pick which to apply. Applying creates a v2 ruleset (published versions are immutable), with the call as its source, and opens a PR-style diff for review. Only after the diff is approved does status flip to verified, with verifiedBy, verifiedOn and the contact.
• The verified badge (W6.5) then shows "Verified with <authority>, <date>" on the coverage page, on sites, on filings, and in the W10.3 county panel.
• Partial verification is allowed and shown: "Report channel verified; fees unconfirmed." Each field in the profile carries its own verified: true/false so the badge can be honest.
W12.3 Call sheet export
• A button on the coverage page: "Call sheet" produces a PDF, one page per draft authority, with the office name, phone, email, hours, the person to ask for if known, and the open questions in priority order (submission channel and signature acceptance first). Justin prints or opens it on his phone before calling.
Done when: all 23 authorities have a ruleset or a documented reason there is none (Kaufman may stay "blocked, nothing readable"); Justin records one real call through the form and a v2 verified ruleset results with no engineer involved; the call sheet PDF exists.
